Decide only whether TEXT asks to discover an association/commonality between the supplied endpoints, rather than the truth or value of the syntactic predicate itself. An association request asks for something shared by the endpoints: a common property, shared event/context, semantic bridge, or memory path. Classify from TEXT semantics, not from the parser's surface predicate label: a commonality question may be represented by an ordinary copular/STATE shell even though its operation is ASSOCIATION. The compared entities are the endpoints; a relation-description/state slot is not an endpoint when two explicit participants are already shown. Choose the supplied ASSOCIATION option only when that shared connection is what the user asks to discover. Choose ORDINARY for a fact check, role/value question, comparison, or mere co-mention. If the operation itself cannot be resolved, choose the supplied uncertainty option (UNKNOWN or UNCLEAR). Do not invent endpoints or meanings.
